About these guidelines

One look, everywhere Bellweather appears

These pages show how to use the Bellweather logo, colours, type and voice so the integration looks and sounds the same in Home Assistant, on GitHub, in documentation and anywhere else.

They are for contributors, writers and anyone who shows Bellweather: in a README, a blog post, a talk or a dashboard screenshot.

The one rule: use the supplied files. Do not redraw, retype or recolour the logo. Every file listed in section 07 is generated from one geometry source, so they always match.

The idea

The weather here and now, measured

Bellweather tells you what the weather is doing at your home, from what was actually measured, and how it will change, from a forecast that keeps score of itself.

The name. A bellwether was the sheep that wore a bell and led the flock; today the word means a leading indicator, something that shows which way things are heading. Bellweather adds the weather: an indicator you can hear and trust.

The mark tells the same story in one shape:

  • a raindrop: the weather at your home, right now;
  • that is also a bell: a signal you notice, with its clapper below;
  • under a weathervane: direction, the forecast, where things are heading.

When. Where Bellweather introduces itself: the top of the README, a site header, a title slide, a social post. Everywhere else, in Home Assistant and next to other logos, use the logo or the badge: the motto is for the first meeting, not for every mention.

The words. Always The weather here and now, measured., exactly as in the files. The motto is part of the artwork: never type it next to the logo, and never change its words, size or colour.

Size. The motto has to stay readable: the badge at least 12 mm high in print and 56 px on screen, which keeps the motto at 7 pt and 11 px. Smaller than that, use the logo. Clear space as for the logo: a quarter of the badge's height on every side.

Construction

The badge

A cobalt square of 120 units with smoothed corners, holding the white bell and the sun vane.

  • The mark fills 80 % of the badge's height, centred on its bounding box.
  • Each corner is a 27-unit arc (22.5 %) eased into the edges: the curve starts 43.2 units from the corner with zero curvature and grows steadily into the arc. A plain circular corner jumps from straight to round in one point, and the eye sees that jump as a kink.
  • The corners are fixed. A sharper, rounder or plain circular corner is a different badge.
  • The sheen on the bell is a cut-out, so the cobalt shows through it.
  • Exported at 256 and 512 px for Home Assistant, and as badge.svg for everything else.

Construction

The logo with the motto

Same width. The motto is set exactly as wide as the wordmark, so the two lines start and end together; that makes its cap height 0.31 of the wordmark's. The badge, the gap and the wordmark keep the logo's sizes.

Spacing. 0.42 cap height from the wordmark's baseline to the motto's cap line. Beside the badge, the two lines sit in the middle of the badge's height (a = a); stacked, the badge is centred 0.6 cap height above the wordmark, the same gap as in the logo.

Type and colour. Outfit Regular (400), no tracking. The motto is #4A5160 on light grounds (8.0 : 1 on white), #B9BCC4 on dark (9.0 : 1) and #E4ECFC on cobalt (4.8 : 1): softer than the wordmark, still readable as text.

Construction

The mark

Grid. 120 × 120 units. The drop-bell is a circle, centre (60, 72), radius 22, with straight tangents up to the tip (60, 24). The circle runs down to y 83, then one short curve turns into a crisp lip at y 97.4.

Vane. A shaft 6 units thick, an arrow head and two tail fins, merged into one shape and scaled to 85 % around the pivot. Its centre of mass sits exactly on the rod's axis: the head is heavier than the tail, so the vane is shifted 3.9 units to the left. Centred on its outline instead, it pulls to the right.

Rounding. Computed on the merged outline. The vane's outer corners are rounded with a 2-unit radius, and every inner joint (fins into shaft, shaft into rod, rod into tip) gets a 2-unit fillet. There are no seams and no ledges.

Colour seam. The sun part ends just above the point where the rod meets the bell's sides, so the joint's fillet stays with the bell.

Sheen. A long, curved highlight on the belly. It is cut out on light grounds and in the badge; on the open mark on dark it is filled with paper, because a cut-out there would read as a groove.

Screens · HEX / RGB

Web, apps, Home Assistant, GitHub, slides, video, and every PNG and SVG. Office and home printers too: send them the RGB file and let the driver convert it.

Coated paper · CMYK coated

Brochures, flyers, stickers and packaging on gloss or silk paper from a print shop. Profile: ISO Coated v2 (FOGRA39), the European standard for coated stock.

Uncoated paper · CMYK uncoated

Letterheads, notebooks and business cards on matt, uncoated paper. Profile: PSO Uncoated ISO 12647.

What ΔE says. The CIEDE2000 difference between the screen colour and what the ink prints; about 1 is the smallest difference visible side by side. Cobalt is brighter on screen than process inks can print, so it comes out a little duller on paper (7–8). Ink prints visibly lighter on uncoated paper (18), which cannot reach a deep navy.

How the values were made. Converted from sRGB with LittleCMS, relative colorimetric with black point compensation, rounded to whole percent; branding/colour_conversion.py recomputes them for any profile. No Pantone colour is defined: where cobalt must match exactly (signage, textiles), pick the nearest spot colour from a physical Pantone guide and record it in this document. Send the press a PDF with the profile named, and ask for a proof.

Bellweather · Brand guidelines05 · Voice
Voice

How Bellweather speaks

  • Say what was measured. Numbers with their source and time beat adjectives.
  • Say what it costs. Every option names its trade-off in the same breath.
  • Explain why. A reason in one clause turns a rule into a choice.
  • Plain words. Short sentences, everyday terms; technical terms only when they are the precise ones.
  • No hype. No exclamation marks, no superlatives we cannot prove.
Write thisNot this
✓Rounded to about 10 km. Weather services learn your area, not your address.
✕Military-grade privacy!
✓7–31 % lower temperature error than ECMWF IFS alone, measured at six airports over 90 days.
✕The most accurate forecast in the world.
✓Off by default. On: one more service learns your nearest airport's code.
✕Turn this on for better results!
✓No official station within 15 km reported in the last 30 minutes, so radar decides.
✕Error: no data.

  • Set only the height; the width follows. Any height from 20 px; 28 px sits well next to body text.
  • Light on light pages, dark on dark ones. The code below picks the right one by itself.
  • Leave a quarter of its height clear around it, and link it to the project page.
  • Do not edit the text, the colours or the proportions.

Where to host it. Copy credit-light.svg and credit-dark.svg to your own site and point the code there. This is the most private way: no third party sees your visitors, and the badge keeps working if the repository moves. Linking straight to the repository's copy also works, but then GitHub serves every view.

Why SVG. Vector, sharp at any size, about 24 KB. The text is drawn as outlines, so it needs no font and looks the same on every site. PNGs at 28 and 56 px are in the kit for places that do not take SVG.

Why it looks like this

  • One accent, not two. An item that differs from its surroundings is remembered better (the von Restorff effect), and emphasis repeated on several elements cancels out. Sun appears in one place: the vane.
  • Light above, dark below. The visual system assumes light comes from above, and darker objects look heavier. A bright vane over a deep bell reads as natural and stable; nothing bright pulls the eye to the bottom.
  • Measured proportions. Sun covers about 15 % of the mark, well below the balance point for yellow and blue, so it stays an accent.
  • Corners without a kink. The badge's corners change curvature continuously, the way a smoothly bent strip does; a circular corner meets the straight edge with a sudden change that shows at large sizes.
  • A badge, because the slots are square. Home Assistant, app grids and browser tabs show icons in squares. The open mark is only 59 % as wide as it is tall; the badge fills the slot and brings its own ground.
  • Works without colour. In greyscale the mark keeps clear steps of lightness, so it does not depend on hue.
  • Blue for trust. Blue is linked with competence in brand research, the right note for a forecast.
