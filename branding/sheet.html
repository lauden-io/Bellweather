Construction

  • Grid: 120 × 120 units. The drop-bell is a circle (centre 60, 72; radius 22) with straight tangents up to the tip (60, 24). It runs down to y 83, then one short curve turns into a crisp lip at y 97.4.
  • Vane: a shaft 6 units thick, an arrow head and two tail fins, merged into one shape and scaled to 85 % around the pivot. Its centre of mass sits exactly on the rod's axis: the head is heavier than the tail, so the vane is shifted 3.9 units to the left. Centred on its outline instead, it pulls to the right.
  • Rounding: computed on the merged outline. Outer corners of the vane are rounded with a 2-unit radius, and every inner joint (fins into shaft, shaft into rod, rod into tip) gets a 2-unit fillet. There are no seams or ledges.
  • Colour seam: the sun part ends just above the point where the rod meets the bell's sides, so the joint's fillet stays with the bell. The two colours overlap by 0.35 units under the top colour, so no hairline of background shows.
  • Sheen: a long, curved highlight on the bell's belly. On light grounds, including the badge, it is a cut-out (true negative space). On the open mark on dark it is filled with paper: a cut-out there would show the dark background and read as a groove, not as light.
  • Badge: a cobalt square, 120 units, corner radius 27 (22.5 %). The white bell with the sun vane fills 80 % of its height, centred on its bounding box.
  • Logo: the badge is 2.1 times the wordmark's cap height (57.1 of 120 units), so the ascenders of B, l, h and t span half the badge. The cap height is centred on the badge, with 0.6 cap height of space before the B. This ratio was chosen after comparing 1.55, 1.8 and 2.1 in use. The wordmark is Outfit Medium (SIL Open Font License), tracking −0.01 em.

Why it looks like this

  • One accent, not two. An item that differs from its surroundings is remembered better (the von Restorff isolation effect), and emphasis repeated on several elements cancels out. Sun appears in one place only: the vane, which points the way, as a bellwether does.
  • Light above, dark below. The visual system assumes light comes from above (Kleffner & Ramachandran, 1992), and darker objects look heavier (Walker, Francis & Walker, 2010). A bright vane over a deep bell reads as natural and stable, and nothing bright pulls the eye to the bottom.
  • Measured proportions. Sun covers 15 % of the open mark. Itten's teaching ratio for a balanced yellow and blue is 3 : 8 (about 27 % yellow), a rule of thumb that has also been measured against. Well below it, yellow is an accent rather than a rival.
  • Grouping. Same colour groups (Gestalt similarity). The bell and clapper share one colour, so they read as one object, the bell. The vane reads as a second object, the weather.
  • A badge, because the slots are square. Home Assistant, app grids and browser tabs all show icons in squares. The open mark is only 59 % as wide as it is tall, so it left about 40 % of the slot empty. The badge fills the slot and carries its own ground, so it looks the same on light, dark and coloured backgrounds.
  • Works without colour. In greyscale both the badge and the open mark keep clear steps of lightness. They do not depend on hue, which matters for colour-blind viewers.
  • Blue for trust. In brand research, blue is linked with competence (Labrecque & Milne, 2012), the right note for a forecast.

Usage

  • The badge is the default wherever an icon or logo appears: the HA icon, the logo, the README, favicons, app grids.
  • Clear space: at least a quarter of the badge's height on every side of the badge and of the logo.
  • Minimum size: the badge works down to 16 px. White on cobalt is 5.7 : 1 and sun on cobalt 3.2 : 1. The open mark needs 32 px or more on light backgrounds: sun on white is only 1.7 : 1, and a thin vane fades.
  • On blue backgrounds the cobalt badge would sink: use the white one-colour mark there.
  • Never: recolour the parts, put sun on the clapper, change the badge's corner radius, stretch or rotate anything, add outlines or shadows, set the wordmark in another typeface, or put the logo on a busy photo without a calm area behind it.
